# Kaiser et al. (2003) — Annotated PEA

> Provided Example Article 1 from Cargill & O'Connor (2009), Ch. 18.
> Citation: Kaiser, B.N. et al. (2003). The soybean NRAMP homologue, GmDMT1,
> is a symbiotic divalent metal transporter capable of ferrous iron transport.
> *The Plant Journal* 35, 295–304.

Use this as a worked example of the AIMRaD conventions. Each block below is
annotated to show which Cargill principle it demonstrates.

## Title — statement form, keywords front-loaded

> "The soybean NRAMP homologue, GmDMT1, is a symbiotic divalent metal
> transporter capable of ferrous iron transport"

- **Statement form** (subject + verb) — gives an explicit result, not a vague
  noun phrase.
- Keywords up front: *soybean*, *NRAMP homologue*, *GmDMT1*.
- Avoids the weak "Evidence of involvement of…" opener.

## Summary (Abstract) — information elements

The abstract runs B → P → M → R → C:

- **B** Iron is an important nutrient in N₂-fixing legume root nodules… (background)
- **P** …we have identified *Glycine max* divalent metal transporter 1 (*GmDmt1*)… (purpose)
- **M** Antibodies raised… functional complementation in yeast… (methods)
- **R** GmDmt1 was able to both rescue growth and enhance ⁵⁵Fe(II) uptake… (results)
- **C** The results indicate that GmDmt1 is a nodule-enhanced transporter
  capable of ferrous iron transport… (conclusion, hedged with "indicate")

## Introduction — five stages, country→city narrowing

| Stage | Example from the Introduction |
|-------|-------------------------------|
| 1 (broad setting) | "Legumes form symbiotic associations with N₂-fixing soil-borne bacteria…" |
| 2 (prior work) | "Two classes of putative Fe(II)-transport proteins… have been identified in plants (Belouchi 1997; Curie 2000; Eide 1996; Thomine 2000)." |
| 3 (gap) | "The proteins involved in this transport have not yet been identified." |
| 4 (purpose) | "In this study, we have identified a soybean homologue of the Nramp family of membrane proteins, GmDmt1;1. We show that GmDmt1;1 is… localised to the PBM." |

- **Country:** iron/nitrogen fixation in legumes
- **Province:** Fe(II)-transport proteins in plants
- **City:** the Nramp homologue GmDmt1;1 in soybean nodules

## Results — figures first, then prose that highlights

> "Antibodies were raised in rabbits against the N-terminal 73 amino acids of
> GmDmt1;1 (Figure 1c). This antiserum was used in Western blot analysis… The
> anti-GmDMT1 antiserum identified a 67-kDa protein on the PBM-enriched nodule
> protein fraction (Figure 3a), but did not cross-react with soluble nodule
> proteins…"

Annotations:
- **Past tense, passive** for the completed procedure.
- **Combined highlight + location**: "(Figure 3a)" inside the sentence.
- **No commentary**: no "interestingly", no interpretation — that is saved for
  the Discussion.

### Verb tense mixing (Task 6.2)

Within the same paragraph, tense shifts to signal meaning:

- *were raised / was used / identified / did not cross-react* → **past** — the
  completed study.
- *is / occurs* → **present** — an always-true statement ("The protein…
  identified… **is** approximately 10 kDa larger than that predicted…").
- *may be related* → **modal** — a hedged comment, foreshadowing the
  Discussion.

## Discussion — claim strength varies with evidence

| Sentence (paraphrased) | Main verb | that-clause verb | Strength |
|------------------------|-----------|------------------|----------|
| "…it **appears** that GmDmt1;1 **has** the capacity to function in vivo as either an uptake or an efflux mechanism…" | appears | has | Moderate |
| "The presence of an IRE motif **suggests** that GmDmt1;1 mRNA **may be stabilised** by the binding of IRPs…" | suggests | may be stabilised | Weak |

The authors do **not** claim "demonstrate" here because the in-vivo direction
of transport was not directly shown. The hedging matches the evidence.

### Diplomatic disagreement

> "It has been suggested that AtNramp has an intracellular localisation
> (Grotz and Guerinot 2002)."

Rather than attacking, the authors let their own localisation data make the
contrast implicitly.

## Methods — passive to avoid "we" repetition

Almost every sentence in *Experimental procedures* is passive. Why: the active
form would put "we" as the subject of nearly every sentence — monotonous.

> "Soybean seeds were inoculated at planting with *Bradyrhizobium japonicum*
> USDA 110 and grown in river sand… Plants were irrigated daily with a
> nutrient solution lacking nitrogen."

Subheadings (*Plant growth*, *Isolation of GmDmt1;1*, *Northern analysis*,
*Antibody generation…*, *Symbiosome isolation…*, *Functional expression in
yeast*) match the order of the Results topics — Strategy 1 for Methods–Results
alignment.

## What to study in this article

- How the **gap** (Stage 3) is signalled without attacking prior work.
- How **figures carry** the localisation evidence; prose only highlights.
- How the **Discussion's** claim strength drops precisely where the evidence
  is indirect.
- The **Title's** statement form — a model for replacing "The effects of…"
  openers.
