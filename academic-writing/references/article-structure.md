# Article Structure (AIMRaD and variations)

> When to read: before drafting any section, to understand how the parts fit
> the hourglass. Source: Cargill & O'Connor (2009), Ch. 2.

## The AIMRaD hourglass

Most scientific research articles follow the **AIMRaD** shape. The width of
each segment represents the **breadth of focus**, not the length.

```
Abstract
Introduction      (broad  ───────────── narrow)
Methods                            (narrow)
Results                            (narrow)
Discussion        (narrow  ───────────── broad)
```

Key features of the hourglass:

1. **The Results box governs everything.** Every part of the article must
   relate to the data and analysis in Results.
2. **The Introduction narrows.** It starts broad (the field's importance) and
   ends at exactly the focus of the Results — usually the aim or principal
   finding.
3. **The Discussion widens.** It starts at the Results' focus and ends at the
   same breadth where the Introduction began, showing the work's place in the
   bigger picture.
4. **Methods establishes credibility** for the Results.

## Variations

| Variation | Layout | When used |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| **AIMRaD** | Abstract → Intro → Methods → Results → Discussion | Default for most journals |
| **AIRDaM** | Abstract → Intro → Results → Discussion → Methods (after, smaller type) | Some molecular-biology journals; Methods demoted to the end |
| **AIM(RaD)C** | Abstract → Intro → Methods → repeated Results+Discussion → Conclusions | Shorter articles; combined Results/Discussion with a closing Conclusions section |

High-prestige journals (*Nature*, *Science*) use compressed conventions with
Methods often only summarised in the main text and full details online. These
are unrealistic targets for most early-career authors; learn the standard
convention first.

## What this means for drafting

- Before drafting, confirm which variation the target journal uses. Check the
  Instructions to Contributors and sample recent articles.
- The Introduction's end-focus must match the Results' focus and the
  Discussion's opening focus. If they diverge, the paper feels incoherent.
- Within the standard AIMRaD, the drafting order is **not** the reading order.
  See `manuscript-process.md` for the recommended sequence (Results → Title →
  Methods → Introduction → Discussion → Abstract).

## Prediction test

A well-structured article lets readers predict which section a sentence comes
from. Test yourself:

| Phrase | Likely section |
|--------|----------------|
| "… yielded a total of …" | Results |
| "The aim of the work …" | Introduction (Stage 4) |
| "… was used to calculate …" | Methods |
| "This may be explained by …" | Discussion |
| "There have been few long-term studies of …" | Introduction (Stage 1/3) |

If a sentence could come from two sections, the structure is not signalling
its purpose clearly enough.
