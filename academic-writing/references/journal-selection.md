# Selecting a Target Journal

> When to read: choosing where to submit. Source: Cargill & O'Connor (2009),
> Ch. 12.

Choose the journal **before** you draft — it determines formatting, length,
audience, and editorial scope. The right journal optimises **speed**,
**prestige**, and **access** for your desired audience.

## Decision criteria

1. **Scope and aims** — Does the journal normally publish work like yours?
   Check several issues. Citing work from the journal in your Introduction
   shows you are "joining a conversation already in progress".
2. **Audience** — Specialist (experts in your field) vs. general (experts from
   other disciplines). This determines how much background to include.
3. **Peer review** — Imperative for international credibility. Confirm the
   journal referees its papers.
4. **Journal Impact Factor** and other indices (see caveats below).
5. **Time to publication** — Check dates on recent articles or the journal
   website. Online-before-print is faster.
6. **Page charges / Open Access** — Some journals charge per page, per colour
   figure, or for OA. Check waivers for your region.
7. **Editorial helpfulness** — Some journals post EAL-targeted author advice;
   ask colleagues about their experiences.

Always have a ranked list of preferred journals so a rejection doesn't cost
weeks of re-decision.

## The Journal Impact Factor — formula and caveats

$$
\text{JIF (Year}_x) = \frac{\text{Cites in }x\text{ to articles published in }(x{-}1)+(x{-}2)}{\text{Number of articles published in }(x{-}1)+(x{-}2)}
$$

Related indices:
- **Immediacy Index** — citations in the same year as publication; measures
  speed of uptake.
- **Cited Half-Life** — how many publication years account for 50% of current
  citations; measures longevity of use.

### Caveats — use indices carefully

- They measure the **average article** in a journal, not yours. Your article's
  citation count may be higher or lower.
- Comparing journals **across fields** is often meaningless (mathematics cites
  few journals; molecular biology cites dozens).
- Indices inflate for journals with review sections vs. original research.
- Sloppy referencing, author-identification problems, and unfamiliar names
  distort citation matching.
- The indexed list largely excludes non-English journals and new journals.
- **Rankings change over time** — a three-year trend is more informative than
  a single year.

The right audience is not always the highest-impact-factor readership.
