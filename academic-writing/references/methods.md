# Writing the Methods section

> When to read: drafting or revising Methods. Source: Cargill & O'Connor
> (2009), Ch. 7.

## Purpose

The Methods section has two jobs:

1. **Reproducibility** — enough detail that a competent peer could repeat the
   work.
2. **Credibility** — enough detail that readers (and referees) can judge
   whether the results mean what you claim.

Referees look here for evidence to answer: *"Do the methods and the treatment
of results conform to acceptable scientific standards?"*

Previously published methods can be cited and summarised; **novel** methods
must be described in full. If the original publication is not in English or
not readily accessible internationally, give the details in your paper too.

## Organisation

Two strategies connect Methods to Results:

- **Strategy 1:** Use identical or similar subheadings in Methods and Results.
- **Strategy 2:** Open each Methods paragraph with a phrase that relates to
  the aims.

> "To generate an antibody to GmDmt1;1, a 236-bp DNA fragment coding for 70
> N-terminal amino acids was amplified using the PCR, …"

The first sentence of each paragraph should introduce the topic and link to
what came before:

> "The disturbance treatment had two levels: control and disturbed. Control
> plots were …" (Britton-Simmons & Abbott 2008)

## Passive vs active voice

Passive voice is common in Methods because the **action** matters more than
the **actor**. But passive is often over-used. Choose based on three factors:

1. **Does the reader need to know who/what did the action?** If not, passive.
   - ✕ "The researchers collected data from all sites weekly."
   - ✓ "Data were collected weekly from all sites."
2. **Does active voice sound repetitive or immodest?** If "we" would be the
   subject of nearly every sentence, passive avoids monotony.
   - "We calculated least significant differences…" → "Least significant
     differences were calculated…"
3. **Does passive improve information flow?** Putting old information first
   sometimes requires passive.

> "We used the results of these analyses to inform the construction of
> mechanistic candidate functions …. *These candidate functions* **were
> compared** using differences in the Akaike information criteria …. We then
> used model averaging …." (Britton-Simmons & Abbott 2008)

Here passive links old info ("These candidate functions") to the start of the
sentence. Promoting flow this way is more important than avoiding passive at
all costs.

## The top-heavy sentence problem

The most common Methods flaw: very long subject + short passive verb at the
end. Readers cannot parse it.

> ✕ "Wheat and barley, collected from the Virginia field site, as well as
>   sorghum and millet, collected at Loxton, were used."
>
> ✓ "Four cereals were used: wheat and barley, collected from the Virginia
>   field site; and sorghum and millet, collected at Loxton."

Note the list structure: short intro clause + `:` + items separated by `;`
(because the items themselves contain commas). Get subject + verb within the
first 9 words; put any list at the end.

## Abbreviating passive sentences

| Original | Abbreviated |
|----------|-------------|
| The data were collected and they were analysed using … | The data were collected and analysed using … |
| The data were collected and correlations were calculated … | The data were collected and correlations calculated … |
| The data which were collected were analysed using … | The data collected were analysed using … |

## What to include (checklist)

- [ ] Overall study design (and justification if non-standard)
- [ ] Population / dataset / materials — inclusion and exclusion criteria
- [ ] Primary endpoint and how it was measured
- [ ] Secondary endpoints
- [ ] Every procedure, tool, version, parameter (hardware, software, configs)
- [ ] Manufacturer/source for specific equipment or datasets
- [ ] Ethical considerations / IRB or equivalent (or statement that none
      applies)
- [ ] Statistical analysis methods, software, sample-size justification,
      pre-specified subgroup analyses
- [ ] For retrospective work: source data first, then criteria, then final N
- [ ] For prospective work: criteria in Methods, but final N belongs in
      Results
