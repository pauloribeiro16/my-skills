# Sentence Templates — Strategic Language Re-use

> When to read: building a repertoire of discipline-specific sentence shells.
> Source: Cargill & O'Connor (2009), Ch. 17.3.

## The concept

A published sentence has two parts:

- a **framework** (the grammatical shell), and
- **noun phrases** (the content chunks, "NP").

Re-using the **framework** with your own content is **not plagiarism** — it is
how scientific English is learned and re-used. Re-using the **content**
without citation is plagiarism.

## Building a template (worked example)

Original (Li et al. 2000):
> "As part of a long-term research effort aimed at establishing a sustainable
> rainfed farming system in the semi-arid and sub-humid regions of northwest
> China, this paper presents a detailed study on the water use patterns and
> agronomic performance for some cropping systems with and without fallow
> crops in a semi-arid environment. The objectives of this study were to:
> (1) determine the grain and aboveground biomass production and water-use
> efficiency of individual crops grown in the rotation; (2) analyze the
> seasonal and inter-annual patterns of soil water storage and utilization as
> well as water stress for the four major rotation crops such as winter wheat,
> corn, potato and millet; (3) determine the grain and aboveground biomass
> production and water-use efficiency for different rotation systems and
> evaluate the capacities of the rotation systems with and without fallow
> crops to utilize soil water storage in conjunction with seasonal
> precipitation; (4) establish whether the introduction of fallow crops into
> the wheat monoculture significantly influences the quantity of water stored
> in the soil that will be used by the subsequent wheat crop; and (5) discuss
> the characteristics of soil conservation for different rotation systems."

Strike out the content noun phrases; what remains is the template:

> "As part of a long-term research effort aimed at ~~[NP1]~~, this paper
> presents ~~[NP2]~~. The objectives of this study were to: (1) determine
> ~~[NP3]~~; (2) analyze ~~[NP4]~~; (3) determine ~~[NP5]~~ and evaluate
> ~~[NP6]~~; (4) establish whether ~~[NP7]~~ significantly influences
> ~~[NP8]~~; and (5) discuss ~~[NP9]~~."

Now reuse the shell with your own NPs.

## Characterising the noun phrases

To judge whether a template fits your meaning, list each NP's grammatical
shape. From the example above:

| NP | Shape |
|----|-------|
| 1 | verb + -ing + NP + in + [NP of location] |
| 2 | *a study* + on + NP + in + [NP of location] |
| 3 | NP + of + [NP referring to features of study already introduced] |
| 4 | NP + for + NP stating subjects of study |
| 6 | *the capacities of* [NP] to + verb + object |
| 7 | *the introduction of* + NP + into + NP |
| 8 | NP of measurement |
| 9 | NP referring to type of conclusions expected |

## A discipline habit

Whenever you read a paper for content, spend 10 extra minutes mining it for
templates. File them by article section (Introduction Stage 4, Methods,
Discussion claim, etc.). Over time you build a repertoire tailored to your
field.

## Common shells

**Stage 4 (Introduction aim):**
```
The objectives of this study were to: (1) [verb] [NP]; (2) [verb] [NP];
(3) evaluate [NP]; and (4) discuss [NP].
```

**Methods paragraph opener linking to aims:**
```
To [verb] [NP], [NP] was/were [method verb] using [NP].
```

**Results highlight + location:**
```
[Measurements/Analysis] of [NP] (Figure N) revealed that [NP] were/are [finding].
```

**Discussion claim — strong:**
```
Our results demonstrate that [NP] [verb-present] [NP].
```

**Discussion claim — hedged:**
```
The presence of [NP] suggests that [NP] may be [verb-past participle] by [NP].
```

## Noun-noun phrases

A second source of re-usable structure. The second element becomes the
headword; the modifying noun moves in front, always **singular**:

| Extended form | Noun-noun phrase |
|---------------|------------------|
| pressure exerted by propagules | propagule pressure |
| success of invasions | invasion success |
| nodules on the roots of legumes | legume root nodules |
| acquisition of iron by bacteroids | bacteroid iron acquisition |

Make a list of the noun-noun phrases used repeatedly in your field, and learn
them accurately — singular vs plural matters.

## Why this works

Science writing is largely sentence **templates** (re-usable across fields)
plus **noun phrases** (specific to your field). Mastering both lets you read
unfamiliar papers by skipping the NPs on first pass, then looking up only the
ones that recur.
